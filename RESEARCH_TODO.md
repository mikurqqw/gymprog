# Evidence review follow-up

Review date: 2026-11-15. This file records verification scope; an unchecked claim is not necessarily false. Existing source data has been preserved.

## Verified sources used in program-evidence.json

- [ACSM 2026 overview of resistance-training reviews](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12965823/): healthy adults, general training principles. No validation of the application's exact templates or injury clearance.
- [ISSN 2017 protein position stand](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28642676/): general protein guidance. Not a trial of the application's menus, calorie deficit or individual targets.
- [Schumann et al. 2022 concurrent-training meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34757594/): maximal strength and hypertrophy generally compatible with concurrent work; explosive-strength interference remains possible. Exact app cardio doses were not tested.
- [2020 powerlifting taper review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7552788/): small and heterogeneous cohorts; no universally optimal taper or validation of this exact cycle.
- [2022 systematic review of arm-wrestling humeral fractures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10426487/): reported injury cases; no incidence estimate, safe side-pressure dose or proof that a training template prevents injury.

The `strength` value is an editorial assessment of support for the stated principle, not a formal GRADE rating and not validation of a generated program. Reviews and position stands are labeled as such; they are not individual randomized trials. Source pages and indexed abstracts were checked. The summaries are original paraphrases, not reproductions or translations of the articles. Kazakh wording and health content still need native-speaker and qualified-clinician review.

## Legacy statements with verified sources but unchecked applications

- Taper: check the legacy author attribution “Pritchard et al., 2020” against the actual review bibliography. Reassess its universal-looking “30–70% over 1–2 weeks” wording: the source includes varied populations and durations. Do not infer that app weeks 7–8 have been experimentally validated.
- ACSM: “practice technique” and the precise eight-week load schedule are coaching applications, not findings of a direct app trial. The review's healthy-adult population does not establish safety after fracture, surgery or active injury.
- ISSN: the app's narrower protein target is an implementation choice. It does not establish safety in kidney disease or other medical restrictions; no supplement requirement follows.
- Concurrent training: “20–25 minutes, 1–2 times weekly” and avoiding pre-squat sprints are practical choices, not the exact intervention proven by this meta-analysis.
- Injury scores and substitute selections are conservative heuristics, not measured joint forces, validated clinical protocols or medical clearance. No reviewed source validates a universal exercise-replacement matrix.

## Remaining research work

- Find direct training-intervention evidence for arm wrestling, including participant experience, adverse-event reporting and measured outcomes. Until verified, retain limited certainty and avoid claims that exact forearm, pronation or side-pressure prescriptions are proven.
- If the product claims fat-loss efficacy, verify a direct source for the actual intervention and distinguish fat loss, lean mass and strength outcomes. Protein guidance alone does not validate a calorie formula, deficit or rate of weight loss.
- Review exact program volume, progression and taper rules with a qualified strength coach. Clearly distinguish general evidence-informed design from a clinically tested program.
- Check future revisions and retractions before updating claims. Verification date is not a guarantee that evidence remains current.
